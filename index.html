01 / The recommendation

Three decisions. Different economic problems.

Electrification and autonomy require separate investment tests.

01 / SUPPORT

Improve human-driver EV economics.

Prioritize reliable charging, affordable rentals, and targeted adoption assistance. Assess earnings after all vehicle costs before promoting a switch.

Remove the binding constraint
02 / TEST

Evaluate FSD separately.

No blanket subsidy for Tesla FSD (Supervised). A voluntary pilot must demonstrate incremental benefits beyond EV savings and the subsidy itself.

Human supervision remains
03 / INTEGRATE

Stage third-party L4 deployment.

Partner within supported operating conditions. Start with verified service gaps, dependable charging, clear responsibilities, and access to performance data.

Expand through evidence gates

The essential distinction: FSD (Supervised) requires an attentive human driver. Driver-out Level 4 operates without an onboard driver within its supported conditions. They do not offer the same labor economics. [2, 3]

02 / What the case establishes

Promising signals. Unanswered questions.

LONDON · CASE P. 5

40% electric miles

By end-2025, 40% of Uber’s London platform miles were electric. Coordinated incentives and charging accompanied adoption; the case does not isolate each intervention’s effect.

AUSTIN · CASE P. 18

50% relative increase

The CEO reports EV trip share rose 50% after the Waymo launch. Illustratively, a 10% share would become 15%. This is not a 50-percentage-point increase or proof of driver benefits.

UTILIZATION · CASE P. 18

20 hours is an expectation

Leadership describes AVs operating 20 hours daily and increasing industrial charging needs. Operating hours do not establish paid utilization, profitability, or displaced jobs.

03 / Who captures the value?

A growing platform can distribute gains unevenly.

These are stakeholder hypotheses to evaluate, not benefits or harms established by the Austin case evidence.

04 / Interactive scenario lab

Test the assumptions behind the recommendation.

All dollar amounts and scenario settings below are illustrative, not case observations, partner terms, or forecasts. Calculations update as you move the sliders.

Does higher utilization pay?

Illustrative fleet economics after platform fees. Fixed cost must include capital and overhead; variable cost must include allocated empty travel, energy, maintenance, and support.

Daily fleet operating result

Daily result = paid trips × (net revenue per trip − variable cost per trip) − fixed daily cost. This is a fleet calculation; Uber’s own margin needs a separate test.

Will human-driver trips hold up?

Normalize starting human-driven trip demand to 100. Compare demand growth with the share of total trips served by autonomous vehicles.

Human-driven trips versus baseline

Human-trip index = 100 × (1 + demand growth) × (1 − AV share). The bar uses a fixed 0–200 index scale; baseline is 100. Preserving trip count does not preserve earnings, trip quality, or every driver’s access to work.

Implication: With a 20% AV share, demand must grow 25% just to preserve total human-driven trip volume.

06 / Rollout discipline

Earn the next stage of expansion.

Proposed evaluation design: four-week baseline, then an initial 12-week pilot with comparable control areas. Account for seasonal demand, spillovers, and driver exits. This window cannot establish rare-event safety on its own.

01 · Safety

Required authorization, reviewed operating evidence, and resolved critical hazards. No-crash pilot results alone are insufficient.

02 · Value

Positive recurring Uber contribution, credible fleet returns, and fewer unfulfilled requests without worse pickup performance.

03 · Driver impact

Pause additions if estimated pilot-related median or bottom-quartile net hourly earnings fall more than 5% versus controls for four weeks.

04 · Environment

Lower emissions per passenger-mile against comparable human-driven EV service; disclose total emissions and vehicle miles.

Pilot windows and the 5% earnings trigger are proposed management choices, not case findings or scientific thresholds. Investigate uncertainty before attributing losses; use verified losses to guide time-limited support and rollout changes.

07 / Evidence, limits, and judgment

Keep the recommendation conditional.

Expand targeted FSD support only if incremental benefits exceed its cost under active supervision. Slow L4 additions when safety, charging, economics, or driver-impact evidence fails the gates. Electric trip growth alone should not override those findings.

Unknowns include Austin partner pricing, fully loaded AV costs, causal demand growth, FSD-specific benefits, and driver earnings effects. The case cannot determine optimal AV fleet size.
